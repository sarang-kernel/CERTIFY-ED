# NONHERMITIAN.md: Bauer-Fike and the residual forms of E-02 on Mathlib

This document describes the Lean 4 formalization of the exact statements behind the non-Hermitian claim kind of CERTIFY-ED (roadmap items E-02 and N-13). The theorems are in `lean/CertifyMathlib/NonHermitian/BauerFike.lean`, the root module is `CertifyMathlib.NonHermitian` (file `CertifyMathlib/NonHermitian.lean`), and the machine-readable record of every theorem, its axioms and the checks is `lean/results/mathlib_nonhermitian.json`. The toolchain is Lean 4.35.0 with Mathlib `v4.35.0`, as in `docs/lean/README.md`.

The statements are taken from the derivation document `docs/proofs/E-02_non_hermitian.md` of the code repository at commit 167940a on the branch `track/non-hermitian`, which starts from `dev/3.2` at fa86456. That branch is not yet part of `archive_v5/`, so paths of the form `docs/...` and `certify_ed/...` in this document are relative to the root of the code repository at commit 167940a. The code is `certify_ed/nonhermitian.py`.

## What the derivation document states

A complex square matrix `A` is non-Hermitian when it differs from its conjugate transpose. Its eigenvalues may then be complex, and its eigenvectors need not be orthogonal or even form a basis. The certifier receives claimed eigenvalues `μ_1, ..., μ_n` and claimed eigenvectors, the columns of a matrix `V`, and forms the residual matrix `R = A V - V diag(μ)`. Below, `‖·‖` is the Euclidean norm of a vector and the spectral norm of a matrix (the largest factor by which the matrix stretches a vector).

The document has four exact results. Lemma 1 (the backward error of a residual) says that a claimed pair `(μ, x)` with `x ≠ 0` and residual `r = A x - μ x` is an exact eigenpair of `A + E` for the rank-one matrix `E = -r x^H / (x^H x)`, whose norm is `‖r‖ / ‖x‖`. Theorem 2 is the theorem of Bauer and Fike (1960): if `A = V D V⁻¹` with `D` diagonal, every eigenvalue of `A + E` lies within `‖V⁻¹ E V‖ ≤ ‖V⁻¹‖ ‖E‖ ‖V‖` of a diagonal entry of `D`; its residual form says that some diagonal entry lies within `‖V‖ ‖V⁻¹‖ ‖A x - μ x‖ / ‖x‖` of any `μ`. Lemma 3 turns a computed inverse `W` into a bound on the exact inverse: if `‖I - W V‖ ≤ θ < 1`, then `W` and `V` are invertible and `‖V⁻¹‖ ≤ ‖W‖ / (1 - θ)`. Theorem 4 joins them for a matrix `V` that is only approximately an eigenvector matrix: with `F = V⁻¹ R` and `ρ ≥ ‖F‖`, part (a) says that `A` is similar to `diag(μ) + F`, part (b) that every eigenvalue of `A` lies in one of the discs of radius `ρ` about the `μ_i`, and parts (c) to (e) count the eigenvalues in each connected group of discs. Theorem 4 does not assume that `A` is diagonalizable. Propositions 5 to 7 of the document bound the floating-point computation of the backward errors, of `‖V⁻¹‖` and of `ρ`. The record statements N1 to N4 of the code rest on Lemma 1, Lemma 3, Theorem 4 (b) and Theorem 4 (c) in that order.

## Why the exact part was formalized

E-02 is new mathematics for the project. Under the decision D-12 it stays UNVERIFIED until Sarang Vehale has re-derived it, and the code marks every statement of the claim kind in that way. Its exact part is short and classical, but the code uses one form of it that textbooks state less often: Theorem 4 (b) for a computed `V` that does not diagonalize `A` exactly, combined with the bound of Lemma 3 from a computed inverse, with the invertibility of `V` obtained from Lemma 3 rather than assumed. A hidden hypothesis in that combination, for instance that `A` is diagonalizable or that `V` is known in advance to be invertible, would leave every radius of statement N3 without support. The machine checks of the document (2696 random and 547 adversarial cases with 0 violations, `docs/proofs/runs/verify_E-02.txt`) test the inequalities on examples and cannot exclude such a gap in the argument. A kernel-checked statement of exactly the combination removes that risk. A failure to state it without additional hypotheses would have required an erratum to the derivation document before the code could leave its UNVERIFIED state. The counting statement and the floating-point accounting were left for later because they need much more groundwork (see the section "What is not formalized, and why").

## What a status means here

The statuses and levels are those of `ENCLOSURE.md`. A statement is "proved" when every Lean theorem named for it is kernel-checked in Lean: it elaborates with no `sorry`, no `native_decide` and no new `axiom`, and `#print axioms` lists only `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`. Such a theorem is at level K. A statement is "partial" when a named part of it is missing, and "not attempted" when no Lean file states it. Level K+ means in addition that the module was replayed and accepted by `leanchecker`; level K2 means that the theorem, exported with its dependencies by `leanexport`, was accepted by the independent kernel `nanoda`. A kernel-checked Lean proof is a formal proof of the statement as written in Lean; the differences from the document's statement are given under "Correspondence". Whether such a proof meets the rule D-12 is Sarang Vehale's decision. No result of this document has been checked by a person.

## The common framework

Each result is first proved for linear maps between normed spaces over a field `𝕜` that is the real or the complex numbers (Mathlib's class `RCLike`), and then for matrices acting on `EuclideanSpace 𝕜 n` (column vectors with the Euclidean norm) through `Matrix.toEuclideanLin`. The index type `n` is any finite type, nonempty where a nearest index is chosen. An eigenvalue `λ` of a matrix is represented by an eigenvector: the hypotheses are `x ≠ 0` and `toEuclideanLin A x = λ • x`. Since every eigenvalue has such a vector, the conclusions hold for every eigenvalue.

Norms of operators are written by their defining bound, as in `ENCLOSURE.md`: "`‖R‖ ≤ r`" is the hypothesis `∀ v, ‖R v‖ ≤ r * ‖v‖`. This is equivalent to a bound on the spectral norm, and it allows any upper bound to be used. The code bounds `‖R‖` by the Frobenius norm (the square root of the sum of the squared moduli of the entries); that the Frobenius norm is such a bound is the inequality `‖R v‖ ≤ ‖R‖_F ‖v‖` (Cauchy-Schwarz row by row), which is not part of this file.

A diagonal map `D` with diagonal `d` enters through `diag_lower`: if `(D w)_i = d_i w_i` for every `w` and `0 ≤ m ≤ |d_i - λ|` for every `i`, then `m ‖w‖ ≤ ‖D w - λ w‖` for every `w`. The proof compares the squared norms coordinate by coordinate (`EuclideanSpace.norm_sq_eq`). The nearest index is chosen by `Finset.exists_min_image`. Three small lemmas translate matrix products into compositions of maps: `toEuclideanLin_diagonal_apply`, `toEuclideanLin_mul_apply` and `toEuclideanLin_one_apply`.

## Correspondence

### Lemma 1

Document: for `x ≠ 0` and any `μ`, the matrix `E = -r x^H / (x^H x)` with `r = A x - μ x` satisfies `(A + E) x = μ x` and `‖E‖ = ‖r‖ / ‖x‖`; hence the smallest singular value of `A - μ I` is at most `‖r‖ / ‖x‖`; the left residual gives the same statement for `A^H`. Lean, `backward_error`: for any linear map `T` on an inner product space over `𝕜`, any `μ` and any `x ≠ 0`, there is a linear map `K` with `T x + K x = μ x` and `‖K v‖ ≤ ‖T x - μ x‖ / ‖x‖ * ‖v‖` for all `v`. The map is the document's `E`, written as `K v = -(⟪x, v⟫ / ‖x‖²) (T x - μ x)` with Mathlib's `innerₛₗ` and `LinearMap.smulRight`; the bound uses the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality `‖⟪x, v⟫‖ ≤ ‖x‖ ‖v‖`. The Lean statement has the upper bound on `‖K‖` where the document has equality; the upper bound is the direction that statement N1 of the code uses. The singular value form, the left residual form and the two by two example of the document are not stated, so the status is partial.

### Theorem 2 and its residual form

Document: if `A = V D V⁻¹` with `D = diag(d)`, every eigenvalue `λ` of `A + E` satisfies `min_i |λ - d_i| ≤ ‖V⁻¹ E V‖ ≤ ‖V⁻¹‖ ‖E‖ ‖V‖`. Lean, `bauerFike_core`: let `T` and `K` be linear maps on `E`, `P` from `F` to `E` with inverse `Q`, and `D` a linear map on `F` with `T P = P D`; if `‖P‖ ≤ c_P`, `‖Q‖ ≤ c_Q`, `‖K‖ ≤ e` and `m ‖w‖ ≤ ‖D w - λ w‖` for all `w`, then every eigenvalue `λ` of `T + K` has `m ≤ c_Q e c_P`. The proof writes the eigenvector as `x = P w` and obtains `D w - λ w = -Q K P w`, then takes norms. The document instead shows that `I + (D - λ I)⁻¹ V⁻¹ E V` is singular and uses the Neumann series; the Lean argument needs neither the case `λ = d_i` nor the series. `bauerFike` is the case of a diagonal `D` on `EuclideanSpace 𝕜 n`, and `bauerFike_matrix` the matrix statement: for `A = V diag(d) W` with `W V = V W = 1` and the bounds `c_V`, `c_W`, `e` of `V`, `W` and `E`, some `i` has `|λ - d_i| ≤ c_W e c_V`. `bauerFike_matrix_sharp` is the first inequality of the document: if `A V = V diag(d)`, `W V = V W = 1` and `‖W E V‖ ≤ f`, some `i` has `|λ - d_i| ≤ f`.

Residual form, document: for every `x ≠ 0` and `μ`, `min_i |d_i - μ| ≤ κ₂(V) ‖A x - μ x‖ / ‖x‖` with `κ₂(V) = ‖V‖ ‖V⁻¹‖`. Lean, `bauerFike_residual` and `bauerFike_residual_matrix`: with the same data, some `i` has `|d_i - μ| ≤ c_V c_W ‖A x - μ x‖ / ‖x‖`. The document derives it from Lemma 1 and the first part; the Lean proof uses `Q (A x - μ x) = D (Q x) - μ Q x` directly. The conclusion is the same.

### Lemma 3

Document: if `‖I - W V‖ ≤ θ < 1`, then `W` and `V` are invertible, `V⁻¹ = (I - G)⁻¹ W` with `G = I - W V`, and `‖V⁻¹‖ ≤ ‖W‖ / (1 - θ)`. Lean, `neumann_bound`: the hypotheses `‖z - W V z‖ ≤ θ ‖z‖` for all `z` and `‖W‖ ≤ c_W` give `(1 - θ) ‖z‖ ≤ c_W ‖V z‖` for all `z` (triangle inequality). `neumann_inverse`: on a finite-dimensional space, with `θ < 1`, there is a linear map `V⁻¹` with `V V⁻¹ = V⁻¹ V = 1` and `‖V⁻¹ y‖ ≤ c_W / (1 - θ) ‖y‖`. The proof obtains injectivity of `V` from `neumann_bound`, surjectivity from finite dimension (`LinearMap.injective_iff_surjective`), and the bound from `neumann_bound` applied to `V⁻¹ y`; it does not use the series. The invertibility of `W` and the formula for `V⁻¹` are not stated, so the status is partial; the code uses only the bound.

### Theorem 4 (b) and its combination with Lemma 3

Document: with `V` invertible, `F = V⁻¹ R` and `ρ ≥ ‖F‖`, every eigenvalue of `A` lies in a disc `D(μ_i, ρ)`. Lean, `similarity_inclusion` (maps) and `disc_inclusion_matrix` (matrices): if `A V = V diag(μ) + R`, `W V = V W = 1` and `‖W R‖ ≤ f`, every eigenvalue `λ` of `A` has `|λ - μ_i| ≤ f` for some `i`. `A` is not assumed to be diagonalizable. The document proves (b) through (a) and Theorem 2 for `diag(μ) + F`; the Lean proof writes the eigenvector as `x = V w` and obtains `diag(μ) w - λ w = -V⁻¹ R w` directly, so part (a) is not needed.

`disc_inclusion_certified` is the combination that the statements N2 and N3 of the code use, with no invertibility hypothesis: for any matrix `W`, if `‖I - W V‖ ≤ θ < 1`, `‖W‖ ≤ c_W`, `A V = V diag(μ) + R` and `‖R‖ ≤ r`, every eigenvalue `λ` of `A` has `|λ - μ_i| ≤ c_W / (1 - θ) * r` for some `i`. In the code (`certify_ed/nonhermitian.py`, `inverse_bound` and `certify_nonhermitian`), `c_W` is a computed upper bound on `‖W‖`, `θ` a computed upper bound on `‖I - W V‖_F`, `β` the quotient `c_W / (1 - θ)` evaluated with upward rounding, and `ρ = up(β ρ_R + 2⁻¹⁰⁷³)` with `ρ_R` an upper bound on `‖R‖_F`, so that `ρ ≥ c_W / (1 - θ) * ‖R‖_F` when the computed bounds are correct. That they are correct is Propositions 6 and 7 of the document, which are not formalized here.

## The checks and their results

Elaboration and build. The file was first elaborated on its own with `lake env lean CertifyMathlib/NonHermitian/BauerFike.lean`, the cheapest way to find errors before a build. The first run reported three errors, all in `backward_error`: the lemma name `innerₛₗ_apply` does not exist in this Mathlib version, so two simplification steps did not fire and one rewrite then failed. The proof now states the application of `innerₛₗ` as a definitional equality (`hin`). The second run reported only linter warnings (a `ring` that was never reached because `field_simp` had closed the goal), and the step was removed. The theorem `bauerFike_matrix_sharp` was added after that run. `lake build CertifyMathlib.NonHermitian` on the final file completed with 2484 jobs, 173 seconds for `BauerFike` and 103 seconds for the root module on a machine shared with other builds (`Drafts/NonHermitian/check_build.log`). Its only warnings are the two notes that the file imports Mathlib modules designed for the module system, the same notes as for the counting files (`results/mathlib_counting.json`).

Axioms and text search. An elaborated file can still depend on an unfinished proof through an imported declaration, and `#print axioms` follows every dependency, so it was run for all sixteen theorems (`Drafts/NonHermitian/PrintAxioms.lean`, output `Drafts/NonHermitian/check_axioms.log`, 227 seconds). Every theorem depends on `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound` and on no other axiom; in particular none depends on `sorryAx`. A text search of the three Lean files (the module, its root and the axiom script) found no `sorry`, `admit`, `native_decide`, `axiom` declaration or `set_option` (`Drafts/NonHermitian/check_textsearch.log`). Every theorem is therefore at level K.

Replay by `leanchecker`. The elaborator and the kernel run in one process during a build, so a fault in how the elaborator hands declarations to the kernel would not be visible at level K. `leanchecker` loads the compiled modules and checks every declaration again in a fresh kernel. `lake env leanchecker CertifyMathlib.NonHermitian.BauerFike CertifyMathlib.NonHermitian` ended with exit code 0 and no output after 2989 seconds (`Drafts/NonHermitian/run_checks_summary.txt`, `Drafts/NonHermitian/check_leanchecker.log`). The same form of command accepted the counting modules (`results/mathlib_counting.json`). Every theorem of the file is at level K+.

Independent kernel `nanoda`. `leanchecker` runs the toolchain's own kernel a second time, so a fault in that kernel would pass both checks above. `nanoda` is a type checker for Lean written independently of it. Six theorems were exported with `leanexport`, each together with every declaration it depends on, the Lean core and Mathlib included, and each export was checked by `nanoda_bin` through the driver `Drafts/Counting/nanoda_counting.py` (one theorem per run, the permitted axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`, a stop at 4096 MiB of private memory or 1800 seconds). The six are `disc_inclusion_certified`, `bauerFike_matrix`, `bauerFike_residual_matrix`, `disc_inclusion_matrix`, `backward_error` and `bauerFike_matrix_sharp` (`Drafts/NonHermitian/nanoda_theorems.txt`). All six were accepted, with 16657 to 23576 declarations per export, export files of 69.8 to 110.2 MB, at most 3711 MiB of private memory for `leanexport` and at most 261 MiB for `nanoda_bin`, and 4650.9 seconds in total (`Drafts/NonHermitian/nanoda_results.jsonl`). Every run also printed the message "Unable to print axioms" from the pretty printer of `nanoda`, as the counting runs did (`results/mathlib_counting.json`); it concerns the printing of the axiom list, not type checking. The other ten theorems are cited by the proofs of these six and are therefore part of their exports, so every theorem of the file is at level K2. The first attempt of this step did not start, because the check script found no Python interpreter on its search path (the line "nanoda exit=0 seconds=1" of `Drafts/NonHermitian/run_checks_summary.txt`, message in `Drafts/NonHermitian/check_nanoda.log`); the script now takes the interpreter as the parameter `-Python`. `bauerFike_matrix_sharp` was added to the list after the first five runs because it is the one matrix statement that is not contained in their exports.

## What is not formalized, and why

Theorem 4 (a) is not stated because the Lean proof of (b) does not need it. Theorem 4 (c) to (e), the count of eigenvalues in each connected group of discs, is not attempted. The document's proof follows the eigenvalues of `diag(μ) + t F` for `t` from 0 to 1 and uses the continuity of the roots of a polynomial in a real parameter, counted with algebraic multiplicity (Kato 1995, Chapter II); a Lean proof would have to build that continuity statement, or a matrix form of Rouché's theorem, before the count could be stated. Statement N4 of the code rests on this part, so it remains supported by the derivation document and its machine checks only. Propositions 5 to 7, the floating-point accounting of `ρ`, of the backward errors and of `β`, are not attempted; the floating-point model of `ENCLOSURE.md` (`FloatModel.lean`) is the natural base for them. The inequality between the Frobenius norm and the spectral norm, which links the code's `‖R‖_F` to the hypothesis `‖R‖ ≤ r`, is classical and is not restated here.
