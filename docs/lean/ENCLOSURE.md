# ENCLOSURE.md: the enclosure theorems of CERTIFY-ED on Mathlib

This document describes the Lean 4 formalization of the enclosure theorems of CERTIFY-ED and of their floating-point accounting. The exact part: Theorem 1 (the residual enclosure), its multi-pair form and its version with the S1 model shift, Theorem 3 (Kato-Temple), Gershgorin's theorem for Hermitian matrices, Weyl's inequality and the model shift of Appendix A, Kahan's inclusion theorem, the polar-factor lemma, Theorem 2 and Theorem 2', the variational statements E-05 and the Cholesky reduction of generalized eigenproblems E-03 with the count by inertia of Lemma 3. The floating-point part: a formal model of binary64 rounding with Higham's lemmas, the Theorem 1 accounting of `residuals` for real and complex data, the same accounting for real data under the underflow-tolerant model of INT-05 with the additive terms of the code, Lemmas A1 to A3 and Theorem A4 of Appendix A with the builder error, the rounding count of the matrix-free products D-02, the floating-point steps of E-05 Lemma 3, and the dyadic interval arithmetic of Lemma 17. The files are in `reverification_lean/CertifyMathlib/Enclosure/`, the root module is `CertifyMathlib.Enclosure` (file `CertifyMathlib/Enclosure.lean`), and the machine-readable record of every statement, every theorem name and its axioms is `reverification_lean/results/mathlib_enclosure.json`. The toolchain is Lean 4.35.0 with Mathlib `v4.35.0`, as in `reverification_lean/README.md`.

Every path of the form `code/...`, `manuscript_v4/...` or `project_docs/...` is relative to `sources/archive_v5/`. The statements are taken from `code/certify-ed/docs/theory.md` (sections 2 and 10.5), `manuscript_v4/sections/s3_math.tex` and the derivation documents in `code/certify-ed/docs/proofs/`: `thm1_fp-accounting.md`, `int05_underflow.md`, `thm2_polar-factor.md`, `thm2prime_eps-star.md`, `appA_model-shift.md`, `D-02_matrix-free.md`, `E-05_variational.md`, `E-03_generalized.md` and `L17_dyadic_exp_ln.md`. The code is `code/certify-ed/certify_ed/` (`enclosure.py`, `fp.py`, `construct.py`, `matrixfree.py`, `ivl.py`).

## What a status means here

Each statement has one of three statuses. A statement is "proved" when every Lean theorem named for it is kernel-checked in Lean: it elaborates with no `sorry`, no `native_decide` and no new `axiom`, and `#print axioms` lists only `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`, the three axioms of Mathlib's classical logic (one theorem, `FP.STree.length_leaves`, uses `propext` alone). In the trust scale of `README.md` such a theorem is at level K. A statement is "partial" when a part of it is kernel-checked in Lean and a named part is missing; the missing part is named in the table and in the results file. A statement is "not attempted" when no Lean file states it. Level K+ means in addition that the module was replayed and accepted by `leanchecker` (the toolchain's own kernel run a second time); level K2 means that the theorem, exported with its dependencies by `leanexport`, was accepted by the independent kernel `nanoda`.

A kernel-checked Lean proof is a formal proof of the statement as written in Lean. The difference between that statement and the document's statement is given for each result below, under "Correspondence". For the floating-point statements the Lean statement is about a model of the code's arithmetic: a structure of hypotheses on an abstract rounding operation, and a description of the computation as a composition of rounded operations. That model, and the claim that it describes what NumPy and the code compute, are part of the correspondence and are not checked by Lean. Whether such a proof meets the project's rule D-12 (new mathematics counts as established only after Sarang Vehale has re-derived it, `project_docs/DECISIONS_LOG.md`) is his decision. Until he takes it, the derivation documents keep their status "derived, machine-checked, independently re-derived (a second derivation written from the statement alone, not a human check), Sarang's check pending". No result of this document has been checked by a person.

## The common framework

All exact theorems are first proved for a symmetric linear map `T` on a finite-dimensional inner product space `E` over a field `𝕜` that is the real or the complex numbers (Mathlib's class `RCLike`), together with an orthonormal basis of eigenvectors with real eigenvalues. This pair is the structure `EigenData` of `Spectral.lean`. For a Hermitian matrix `A` the map is `Matrix.toEuclideanLin A` on `EuclideanSpace 𝕜 n` (column vectors with the Euclidean norm), and the eigenbasis is Mathlib's `Matrix.IsHermitian.eigenvectorBasis` with the eigenvalues `Matrix.IsHermitian.eigenvalues` (`hermitianEigenData`), or the sorted basis with the eigenvalues `eigenvalues₀` in decreasing order (`sortedEigenData` in `Weyl.lean`). The reason for this choice is that every proof of the documents is a computation in the eigenvector basis or a dimension count, and both are short for an abstract eigenbasis; the matrix statements are then one line each.

`Spectral.lean` holds the coordinate formulas (Parseval, the residual norm and the Rayleigh quotient in the eigenvector basis) and one counting lemma, `EigenData.finrank_le_card_of_support`: if no nonzero vector of a subspace `Y` has all its coefficients with index in a set `s` equal to zero, then the dimension of `Y` is at most the size of `s`. Its proof intersects `Y` with the span of the remaining eigenvectors (`exists_ne_zero_mem_inf`, from the dimension formula for the sum and the intersection of two subspaces). The two uses are the Courant-Fischer counts: a subspace on which `‖T y - c y‖ ≤ r ‖y‖` forces at least `dim Y` eigenvalues, counted with multiplicity, within `r` of `c` (`finrank_le_card_near`), and a subspace on which the Rayleigh quotient is at most `t` forces at least `dim Y` eigenvalues at most `t` (`finrank_le_card_le`, strict form `finrank_le_card_lt`). Weyl's inequality and Step 1 of Kahan's theorem are instances.

Norms of operators are written by their defining bound: "`‖R‖ ≤ nR`" is the hypothesis `∀ z, ‖R z‖ ≤ nR * ‖z‖`. This is equivalent to a bound on the spectral norm, and it allows any upper bound on the spectral norm to be used, as `manuscript_v4/sections/s3_math.tex` allows ("any upper bound on the spectral norm may be substituted"). For the model shift there is also a form with Mathlib's spectral norm of matrices (`Matrix.Norms.L2Operator`).

## The floating-point model

`FloatModel.lean` states the model as hypotheses, not as an implementation. `Rounding V` is a rounding `rnd : V → V` on a normed group (`ℝ` for real data, `ℂ` for complex data) with a predicate `IsFloat` of representable values, a unit roundoff `u ≥ 0` and an absolute term `eta ≥ 0` such that every result is representable, `‖rnd z - z‖ ≤ u ‖z‖ + eta` for every exact result `z` (the underflow-tolerant model M-U of `int05_underflow.md`), and `‖rnd (a + b) - (a + b)‖ ≤ u ‖a + b‖` for representable `a` and `b` (a sum that underflows is exact). The standard model of `thm1_fp-accounting.md` is the case `eta = 0`. `Binary64` fixes `u = 2⁻⁵³` and `eta = 2⁻¹⁰⁷⁵` and adds monotonicity and the two neighbours `succ` and `pred` of a representable number with `z ≤ succ (rnd z)` and `pred (rnd z) ≤ z`; `outward_interval` is the enclosure property of `fp.add_up` and `fp.sub_down`. The model is consistent: the identity map with `u = 2⁻⁵³` and `eta = 0` satisfies every hypothesis.

Higham's Lemma 3.1 is `higham_lemma_3_1`: a product of `k` factors `(1 + δ_i)^(±1)` with `|δ_i| ≤ u` and `k u < 1` is `1 + θ` with `|θ| ≤ γ_k`, `γ_k = k u / (1 - k u)` (`gam`). The algebra of Lemma 3.3 is `gam_succ` (`γ_k + u (1 + γ_k) ≤ γ_{k+1}`), `gam_add` (`γ_j + γ_k + γ_j γ_k ≤ γ_{j+k}`), `mul_gam_le`, `gam_mono` and `gam_div_one_sub`. A sum evaluated in any order and association is a binary tree `STree` whose leaves are the terms; `STree.comp_error` bounds the computed tree sum when every computed term is within `γ_a ‖t_i‖ + β` of the exact one, which gives the dot product bound of Higham's eq. (3.4) with the INT-05 term (`dot_error`, Lemma U1: `γ_k ∑ ‖a_i b_i‖ + k (1 + γ_{k-1}) eta` for `k` terms) and, for `eta = 0`, the row bound `|fl(A x)_i - (A x)_i| ≤ γ_w (|A| |x|)_i` (`matvec_error`). For complex data, `crnd` rounds both components of a complex result (a complex sum, or a complex number times a real one), which is a rounding on `ℂ` of relative error `u` (`cRounding`); `cmul` forms `(ac - bd) + i(ad + bc)` from four rounded real products and two rounded sums, and `cmul_err` is `|fl(ab) - ab| ≤ γ₃ |ab|` (Higham's Lemma 3.5 in the form `√2 γ₂ ≤ γ₃`, proved as `2 (2u + u²)² ≤ (3u)²`).

Two modelling choices are part of every floating-point statement below. Products are rounded separately and then summed (no fused multiply-add), and the quantity `k u` inside `fp.gamma` is exact (an integer times a power of two). The tree hypothesis covers every order of summation that NumPy, a BLAS library or `np.add.at` may use.

## Correspondence

### Theorem 1 and its two forms

Document (`theory.md` §2): for `x ≠ 0` and real `μ`, some eigenvalue satisfies `|λ - μ| ≤ ‖Ax - μx‖/‖x‖`. Lean, `residual_enclosure`: for a Hermitian `A : Matrix n n 𝕜`, real `μ` and `x : EuclideanSpace 𝕜 n` with `x ≠ 0`, there is an index `i` with `|hA.eigenvalues i - μ| ≤ ‖toEuclideanLin A x - μ • x‖ / ‖x‖`. The only difference is that the field may be the real or the complex numbers. The proof writes the squared residual as `∑ (λ_i - μ)² |c_i|²` and compares it with `r² ∑ |c_i|²`.

The multi-pair form, `residual_enclosure_family`, gives one index per claimed pair. The index map is not claimed to be injective, which is the document's remark that Theorem 1 gives existence only: two copies of one vector are enclosed by the same eigenvalue.

The version with the S1 model shift, `residual_enclosure_of_shift`, encloses an eigenvalue of a Hermitian `H` with `‖(H - A) v‖ ≤ σ ‖v‖` for all `v` (and `residual_enclosure_of_l2_opNorm` with `‖H - A‖ ≤ σ` in Mathlib's spectral norm) by the radius `‖Ax - μx‖/‖x‖ + σ`, computed with the stored matrix `A`. The document's radius is `add_up(r, σ)`, the same sum stepped one unit outward (`Binary64.le_addUp`). The Lean proof uses the triangle inequality and Theorem 1 for `H`, so it needs neither Weyl's inequality nor symmetry of `A`; the version that keeps the sorted index is Theorem A5 below.

### Theorem 1 in floating point, real data

Document (`thm1_fp-accounting.md`): the radius `rel` returned by `residuals` is an upper bound of `‖Ax - μx‖/‖x‖`. Lean (`FloatAccounting.lean`) follows the computation one rounding at a time. The computed product `ŷ_i` is the rounded products `A_ij x_j` summed along a tree `T i` with at most `w` leaves that contain the stored entries of row `i` (`matvecFl`, `RowTrees`). The computed residual is `R̂_i = fl(ŷ_i - fl(x_i μ))` (`residFl`). The entrywise bound is `Ê_i = fl(fl(fl(fl(fl(â_i / fl(1 - γ̂_{w+1})) (1 + 4u)) + fl(|x_i| |μ|)) γ̂_{w+2}) (1 + 4u))` (`roundBoundFl`), with `â_i` the computed `|A| |x|` along a second tree and `γ̂_k = fl(k u / fl(1 - k u))` (`gamHat`, `fp.gamma`). `fp.norm2_upper` and `fp.norm2_lower` are `fl(fl(fl(s fl(1 + γ̂_{n+2})) (1 + 8u)) + T)` and `max(0, fl(fl(fl(s fl(1 - γ̂_{n+2})) / (1 + 8u)) - T))` with `s = fl(√(∑ fl(v_j²)))` along a tree over all entries and arbitrary tails `T ≥ 0` (`norm2Up`, `norm2Low`). The radius is `rel = fl(fl(fl(fl(fl(R_up + E_up) + c₁) / x_low) (1 + 4u)) + c₀)` with `c₀, c₁ ≥ 0`, which allows the code's additive terms (`residual_additive_term` and `2 ULP_MIN`) as arbitrary nonnegative additions (`residualRadiusFl`); in the underflow-tolerant model below they are the computed values of the code.

`residual_radius_fl`: in the standard model (`eta = 0`) with `u ≤ 2⁻⁵³`, `1 ≤ w ≤ 2²⁶` and `n ≤ 2²⁶`, if `x_low > 0` then `‖Ax - μx‖/‖x‖ ≤ rel`; `residual_enclosure_fl` adds Theorem 1 for a real symmetric matrix. The proof has the document's steps: `|R̂_i - r_i| ≤ γ_{w+1} (a_i + q_i)` with `a = |A| |x|` and `q_i = |x_i| |μ|` (`resid_le_roundBound`), `Ê_i` above it (the computed `|A| |x| / (1 - γ̂_{w+1}) (1 + 4u)` is at least `a_i`, then the factor `γ̂_{w+2} ≥ γ_{w+2} (1 - u)²` covers `γ_{w+1}` after six roundings), `norm2_upper(v) ≥ (1 + u) ‖v‖` and `norm2_lower(v) ≤ (1 - u) ‖v‖` (`norm2Up_ge`, `norm2Low_le`, from `|fl(∑ v_j²) - ∑ v_j²| ≤ γ_n ∑ v_j²` and `γ_n + 2u ≤ γ_{n+2}`, `γ_{n+2}² ≤ u`), and the final quotient, where the slack `1 + u` of `norm2_upper` absorbs the fourth rounding, as the document states for step 5. Each numeric step is a separate lemma over real numbers (`num_norm_up`, `num_norm_low`, `num_abs`, `num_E`, `num_final`, with `gam_regime` and `small_consts` for the bounds `k u ≤ 10⁻⁷`, `γ_k ≤ 10⁻³` and `γ_k² ≤ u` when `k ≤ 2²⁶ + 2`). The bounds `w, n ≤ 2²⁶` are hypotheses of the Lean statement; the code accepts any `k` with `k u < 1/2`.

### Theorem 1 in floating point, complex data

`residual_radius_fl_c` and `residual_enclosure_fl_c` (`FloatAccounting.lean`) have the same structure with the constants of `enclosure.py` for complex data. A complex product is `cmul` (error `γ₃`), complex sums and the product `x_i μ` are rounded componentwise, and each modulus `|A_ij|`, `|x_j|`, `|x_i|` is one rounding of the exact modulus (the code computes it with `hypot`; that `hypot` has relative error at most `u` is a modelling hypothesis). Then `|ŷ_i - (A x)_i| ≤ γ_{w+2} (|A| |x|)_i` (`matvecC_error`), the computed `|A| |x|` is at least `(1 - γ_{w+2})` times the exact one (`absMatvecC_ge`) and is divided by `1 - γ̂_{w+3}` as in the code, the residual error is at most `γ_{w+3} (a_i + q_i)` (`residC_entry_err`), and the entrywise bound with `fl(2 γ̂_{w+6})` (`gamma_dot`) is above it (`roundBoundC_ge`). The norm of a complex vector of length `n` is the sum of the `2n` squares of its real components (`comps`), and the factor is still `1 ± γ̂_{n+2}` of `fp.norm2_upper`; the bounds `norm2UpC_ge` and `norm2LowC_le` use `√(1 - g) ≥ 1 - g/2 - g²/4` and `√(1 + g) ≤ 1 + g/2` for `g = γ_{2n}` and `(n u)² ≤ u/2`. The hypotheses are `w + 4 ≤ 2²⁶` and `n ≤ 2²⁶`.

### Theorem 1 in floating point under the underflow-tolerant model (INT-05)

Document (`int05_underflow.md`, Theorem U5): under the model M-U, `fl(z) = z(1 + δ) + η` with `|δ| ≤ u` and `|η| ≤ u_min = 2⁻¹⁰⁷⁵`, the radius returned by `residuals`, with the additive terms of the code, bounds `‖Ax - μx‖/‖x‖` for every input. Lean (`FloatUnderflow.lean`) uses the `Rounding` of `FloatModel.lean` with the absolute term `eta` and the structure `UModel`: `u = 2⁻⁵³`, `eta ≤ 2⁻¹⁰⁷⁵`, no absolute term for an exact result of magnitude at least `2⁻¹⁰²²` (`normalMin`, the normal range), and representable numbers closed under negation. The computation is the one of the real-data accounting above, with the same summation trees and the same order of operations, every rounding now with its absolute term. The additive terms are the computed values of `fp.py`, every operation rounded: `T_N = norm_underflow_term(N) = fl(√(N/2) 2⁻⁵³⁷ (1 + 2γ̂_{N+2}) (1 + 8u)(1 + 8u))` (`normUnder`), added by `norm2_upper` to `R_up` and `E_up` and subtracted by `norm2_lower` in `x_low`; `A_{n,w} = residual_additive_term(n, w) = fl(ldexp(fl(√n c_w (1 + 8u)(1 + 8u)), -1075) + ULP_MIN)` with the computed `c_w = residual_entry_count(w)` (`addTerm`, `cwHat`), added to `R_up + E_up`; and `2 ULP_MIN = 2⁻¹⁰⁷³`, added to the quotient `fl(fl(upper / x_low)(1 + 4u))`. `residual_radius_int05`: with `1 ≤ w ≤ 2²⁶`, `n ≤ 2²⁶` and `x_low > 0`, `‖Ax - μx‖/‖x‖ ≤ rel`, with no hypothesis on the magnitudes of `A`, `x` and `μ`; `residual_enclosure_int05` adds Theorem 1 for a real symmetric matrix.

The terms suffice for four reasons, each a lemma. Lemma U4 holds as `|R̂_i - r_i| ≤ Ê_i + c_w eta` with the exact constant `c_w = (w + 1)(1 + γ_{w+1}) + 2γ_{w+2} w (1 + γ_w)(1 + 4u) + 3γ_{w+2}(1 + 4u)² + 2(1 + 4u)` (`resid_le_roundBound_U`, `cwExact`), which is the formula of `residual_entry_count`. The computed `A_{n,w}` is at least `√n c_w (1 + u) u_min` (`addTerm_ge`): the computed `c_w` is bounded below term by term (`cwHat_ge`, `cw_bridge`), and `ULP_MIN` covers the rounding of `ldexp` to the subnormal grid. The bounds `norm2_upper(v) ≥ (1 + u) ‖v‖` and `norm2_lower(v) ≤ (1 - u) ‖v‖` hold for vectors of any magnitude, `T_N` covering the squares lost to underflow (`normUnder_ge`, `norm2Up_ge_U`, `norm2Low_le_U`). Finally `2⁻¹⁰⁷³` covers the absolute terms of the division and of the product by `1 + 4u` (`chain_final_U`).

One step of the document's proof does not hold as written. It bounds the computed `t = fl(√n c_w (1 + 8u)²)` below by `√n c_w (1 - u)^k (1 + 8u)² ≥ √n c_w (1 + 10u)` for the `k ≤ 12` roundings of `residual_entry_count` and the product, but `(1 - u)¹² (1 + 8u)²` is about `1 + 4u`. The conclusion is not affected: the proof needs only `A_{n,w} ≥ √n c_w (1 + u) u_min`, which `addTerm_ge` proves. The complex-data version of Theorem U5 (`residual_entry_count` with `4w + 2` real products) is not formalized; the complex accounting above is in the standard model. That binary64 arithmetic and NumPy satisfy `UModel` (in particular that a sum of two binary64 values that underflows is exact, and that `ldexp` rounds to the subnormal grid with an error of at most `u_min`) is part of the correspondence.

### Theorem 3 (Kato-Temple)

Document: with `‖x‖ = 1`, `θ = xᴴAx`, `r = ‖Ax - θx‖` and an interval `(α, β)` containing `θ` and exactly one eigenvalue `λ`, `θ - r²/(β - θ) ≤ λ ≤ θ + r²/(θ - α)`. Lean, `kato_temple`: the same, with `θ = re ⟪x, A x⟫` (the lemma `inner_apply_self_eq_re` shows that the quadratic form of a Hermitian matrix is real, so this is `xᴴAx`) and "exactly one eigenvalue" written as "every eigenvalue in `(α, β)` equals `λ`". The eigenvalue `λ` may therefore have any multiplicity. The operator forms are more general: `EigenData.kato_temple_upper` needs only `α < θ` and `EigenData.kato_temple_lower` only `θ < β`, `λ` may be any real number in `[α, β]` that every eigenvalue equals or avoids `(α, β)`, and the division-free forms `(λ - θ)(θ - α) ≤ r²` and `(θ - λ)(β - θ) ≤ r²` need no hypothesis on `θ`. The proof is the classical identity `∑ w_i (λ_i - λ)(λ_i - a) = r² + (θ - λ)(θ - a)` with weights `w_i = |⟨b_i, x⟩|²` (`kato_temple_identity`), whose left side is nonnegative for `a = α` and `a = β`.

### Gershgorin

### Kahan's inclusion theorem

Document (`thm2_polar-factor.md` §1 and §2): for `Q` with orthonormal columns, `H` Hermitian with eigenvalues `θ_1 ≤ … ≤ θ_g` and `r = ‖AQ - QH‖`, there are distinct indices `i_1, …, i_g` with `|λ_{i_j} - θ_j| ≤ r`; `H` need not be `QᴴAQ`. Lean, `kahan` (eigenvalues in increasing order) and `kahan_antitone` (Mathlib's decreasing order): for an isometry `Q` the conclusion is a strictly increasing index map, which is injective and therefore gives distinct indices; it is stronger than the document's statement. Step 1, the interval lemma (`kahan_interval_count`: `#{θ_l ∈ [a, b]} ≤ #{λ_i ∈ [a - r, b + r]}`), follows the document: it is the Courant-Fischer count for the image under `Q` of the eigenvectors of `H` with eigenvalues in `[a, b]`. Step 2 differs. The document builds a bipartite graph and applies Hall's marriage theorem after splitting a union of intervals into connected components. The Lean proof uses an explicit matching on the line instead (`exists_strictMono_matching`): the index of `θ_j` is `p_j = max_{l ≤ j} (#{i : λ_i < θ_l - r} + j - l)`, and the interval lemma for the window `[θ_l, θ_j]` shows `λ_{p_j} ≤ θ_j + r`. The reason for the change is that the explicit matching needs only counts of sorted sequences, where Hall's theorem needs the component decomposition, which is long to formalize; the explicit matching also yields an order-preserving pairing.

For (ii), the quotient `X / Y` for `0 ∉ Y`: the four corner quotients `x_i 2ᴾ / y_j`, floored and ceiled, enclose `x / y` (`div_mem_corners`, `grid_div_mem`). For (iv), the upper atanh sum with its tail term `⌈⌈8 pw_{M+1}/7⌉/(2M+3)⌉` is at least `2ᴾ (log(1 + x) - log(1 - x))` for `x = u 2⁻ᴾ ≤ 0.35` (`pwUp_ge`, `atanh_tail_le` with the tail bound `8/7` for `x² ≤ 1/8`, `lnSeriesUp_ge`); `L2 = (A↓(⌊2ᴾ/3⌋), A↑(⌈2ᴾ/3⌉))` contains `2ᴾ ln 2` for `P ≥ 6` (`ln2_mem`; the code has `P = 192`); the grid arguments `u_lo` and `u_hi` bracket `2ᴾ (m - 1)/(m + 1)` and `u_hi ≤ 0.35 · 2ᴾ` (`u_grid_bounds`); and for `z = 2^k m` the interval `(A↓(u_lo), A↑(u_hi)) + k · L2`, with `k · L2` the exact `scale_int` (ends swapped for `k < 0`), contains `2ᴾ log z` (`ln_point_mem`). For (v), with a set `F` of reals standing for the finite binary64 values, a nearest element `f` of `F` to `q` and `nextafter` given as the neighbour of `f` in `F`, `to_float_down` returns the largest element of `F` at or below `q` and `to_float_up` the smallest at or above (`to_float_down_spec`, `to_float_up_spec`). Not formalized: the bit-length decomposition `v 2⁻ᴾ = 2^k m` of `_ln_point` with `m_lo ≤ m 2ᴾ ≤ m_hi ≤ 2 · 2ᴾ`, which enters `ln_point_mem` and `u_grid_bounds` as hypotheses; the binary64 facts of (v) (correct rounding of `float(Fraction)`, `nextafter` the adjacent binary64), which enter as hypotheses on `F`; and the argument guards of `exp_interval`. One statement of the document is not exact: `from_float` is exact (width 0) only when `f 2ᴾ` is an integer. For a binary64 below the grid resolution, for example `2⁻¹⁰⁷⁴` with `P = 192`, `from_fraction` returns `(0, 1)` (`from_fraction_tiny`, `two_pow_neg_1074_grid`); the enclosure property is unaffected. The status is partial.

## What is not formalized, and why

The parts named as missing above fall into three groups. Code-level evaluations of a bound whose mathematical form is proved: the evaluation of `Δ_builder` in Theorem A4, the computed `E_N`, `r_stored` and Temple rounding of E-05 Lemma 3, and the rounding of `norm_bound()` in D-02. Statements with a combinatorial or algebraic core that is long to formalize: Lemmas M1 and M3 of D-02, Propositions 1 to 3 of E-03 (the Bunch-Kaufman count), Lemma 4 of E-05 (MPS contraction), and the bit-length decomposition of `_ln_point` in Lemma 17. Floating-point statements not yet carried over to the underflow-tolerant model: Theorem U5 for complex data, and D-02 (b), which is proved for real data in the standard model.

## How the files were checked

Each file was elaborated with `lake env lean` until it gave no error, then built as a module, and the root module was built with `lake build CertifyMathlib.Enclosure`. The fourteen files contain no `sorry`, no `admit`, no `native_decide` and no `axiom` declaration; this was checked by searching the files. `#print axioms` was run for every theorem of the fourteen files by `reverification_lean/Drafts/Enclosure/PrintAxioms.lean` (355 theorems); 354 depend exactly on `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`, and `FP.STree.length_leaves` on `propext` alone. Every theorem is therefore at level K. All fourteen modules and the root module were replayed by `leanchecker` and accepted (exit code 0, no output): the first eight modules with the root module in 2541.8 s, `Generalized` in 233.5 s, `FloatModel`, `FloatAccounting`, `MatrixFree`, `ExpLn`, `Generalized`, `Variational` with the root module in 1956.2 s, and after the edits of this round `FloatAccounting`, `FloatUnderflow`, `MatrixFree`, `ExpLn`, `Generalized` with the root module in 1324.4 s. Every theorem is therefore at least at level K+. The independent kernel `nanoda` was run on the 63 main theorems named in the table (15 of them added in this round), one theorem per run, each exported by `leanexport` together with every declaration it depends on (Lean core and Mathlib included) and checked with only `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound` permitted. 62 were accepted in these runs (between 7004 and 35475 declarations per export, 40 to 799 s per theorem for export and check, 105 s at the median). The export of `FP.residual_enclosure_int05` by `leanexport` first went over the limit of 4 GiB of memory set for this check (4099 MiB, and 4101 MiB in a second run); after the final build of the three libraries it was run again with a limit of 6 GiB (`Audit/run_final_checks.py`), needed 4099 MiB, and nanoda accepted it (34948 declarations), so all 63 main theorems are accepted and the statement of Theorem U5 in the table is at level K2. The 48 earlier runs used the build before the edits of this round, which add declarations at the ends of the files, the import of `CertifyMathlib.Counting.Inertia` in `Generalized` and documentation, and leave the checked declarations as they were; the fourth `leanchecker` run covers the current build. The accepted theorems are at level K2; a statement of the table is at level K2 when all its main theorems were accepted. `leanexport` needed between 3275 and 4038 MiB of memory for the accepted exports, almost all of it for loading the imported Mathlib modules; `nanoda` itself needed at most 512 MiB. nanoda reports one pretty printer error ("Unable to print axioms") in every run, which is not a type checking error. The counts, the per-theorem axioms, the build command and its time, and the second-checker runs with their times are in `reverification_lean/results/mathlib_enclosure.json`.

## Recreating the check

In a Windows command window (`cmd.exe`), with the environment of `reverification_lean/README.md` (the Lean binaries and Git on `PATH`, `MATHLIB_CACHE_DIR` set), from the folder `reverification_lean`:

```
lake build CertifyMathlib.Enclosure
lake env lean Drafts/Enclosure/PrintAxioms.lean
lake env leanchecker CertifyMathlib.Enclosure.FloatModel CertifyMathlib.Enclosure.FloatAccounting
lake env leanexport CertifyMathlib.Enclosure.Residual -- CertifyMathlib.Enclosure.residual_enclosure > export.ndjson
nanoda_bin config.json
```

The first command builds the fourteen modules and the root module; the second prints the axioms of every theorem; the third replays two of the modules with `leanchecker`, and any list of module names may be given. The fourth and fifth commands check one theorem with `nanoda`: `leanexport` writes the theorem and its dependencies to `export.ndjson`, and `config.json` contains `{"export_file_path": "export.ndjson", "use_stdin": false, "permitted_axioms": ["propext", "Classical.choice", "Quot.sound"], "unpermitted_axiom_hard_error": true, "nat_extension": true, "string_extension": true, "print_success_message": true}`; the accepting run prints "Checked N declarations with no typechecker errors". The export file of a theorem that uses the spectral theorem is about 200 MB. The times of the runs on the shared build machine are recorded in the results file.
