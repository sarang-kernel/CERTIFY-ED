# COUNTING.md: counting, assembly and eigenspace statements of CERTIFY-ED on Mathlib

This document describes the Lean 4 formalization of the statements of CERTIFY-ED that count eigenvalues, put counts together and bound eigenspaces: Sylvester's law of inertia, Lemma 4 (inertia with backward error), Corollary 5 (completeness of a 'lowest' claim and the sorted pairing), D-01 (the a-posteriori backward error of the sparse factorization, with its floating-point parts), the spectrum of a block-diagonal matrix and Theorem 8 (sector assembly, with the polar factor), Theorem 6 (the projector bound) and the lemmas and propositions of E-07, part (b) of C-12 and C-13, Proposition 14 (degeneracy bracketing), Proposition 15 with its floating-point corollary, Proposition 16 with the shift identity, C-06, C-04, C-07 and E-04, and the sorted-index forms of the counting statements. The files are in `lean/CertifyMathlib/Counting/`, the root module is `CertifyMathlib.Counting` (file `CertifyMathlib/Counting.lean`), and the machine-readable record of every statement, every theorem name and its axioms is `lean/results/mathlib_counting.json`. The toolchain is Lean 4.35.0 with Mathlib `v4.35.0`, as in `docs/lean/README.md`.

Every path of the form `code/...`, `manuscript_v4/...` or `project_docs/...` is relative to `archive_v5/`; `code/certify-ed/` is the clone of the code repository on branch `dev/3.2` at commit `a814d28`. The statements are taken from `code/certify-ed/docs/theory.md` (sections 3, 4, 5, 9 and 10), `manuscript_v4/sections/s3_math.tex` and the derivation documents in `code/certify-ed/docs/proofs/` named in the table below. The floating-point statements use the standard model of the enclosure part of this repository, `CertifyMathlib.Enclosure.FloatModel` and `CertifyMathlib.Enclosure.FloatAccounting` (described in `ENCLOSURE.md`), which the counting files import and do not change.

## What a status means here

Each statement has one of three statuses. A statement is "proved" when every Lean theorem named for it is kernel-checked in Lean: it elaborates with no `sorry`, no `admit`, no `native_decide` and no new `axiom`, and `#print axioms` lists only `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`, the three axioms of Mathlib's classical logic. A statement is "partial" when the core is kernel-checked in Lean and a named part is missing; the missing part is named below and in the results file. A statement is "not attempted" when no Lean file states it, and the reason is given.

The levels are those of the trust scale in `docs/project/README.md`. Level K: a Lean theorem accepted by the Lean kernel, with no `sorry` and no `native_decide`, its axioms printed; every theorem of this part is at level K. Level K+: a level K theorem whose module `leanchecker` replays; this re-runs the same Lean kernel on the compiled files and guards against elaborator and file corruption, not against a kernel defect. Every module of this part and the root module were replayed and accepted, so every theorem is at least K+. Level K2: a level K theorem that the independent kernel `nanoda` (a separate implementation of Lean's type theory, run on the exported declarations) also accepts. `nanoda` was run on the main theorems of each statement (column "Lean theorems (main)" below), one theorem per run, and a statement is at level K2 when all its main theorems were accepted; the result of every run is in the results file.

A kernel-checked Lean proof is a formal proof of the statement as written in Lean. The difference between that statement and the document's statement is given for each result below, under its own heading. Whether such a proof meets the project's rule D-12 (new mathematics counts as established only after Sarang Vehale has re-derived it, `project_docs/DECISIONS_LOG.md`) is his decision. Until he takes it, the derivation documents keep their status "derived, machine-checked, independently re-derived (a second derivation written before the original proof was read, not a human check), Sarang's check pending". No result of this document has been checked by a person, and neither have the correspondence notes.

## Why the library builds its own counting argument

Every counting statement of CERTIFY-ED rests on one fact: the number of eigenvalues of a Hermitian matrix `A` below `t` is the largest dimension of a subspace on which the form `xᴴ (A - t I) x` is negative. Sylvester's law, Weyl's inequality, Lemma 4 and the union of block spectra all follow from it in a few lines. Mathlib `v4.35.0` has the spectral theorem for Hermitian matrices (`Matrix.IsHermitian.spectral_theorem`, with eigenvalues `hA.eigenvalues` indexed by the index type of the matrix) and the existence half of Sylvester's law for real quadratic forms (`QuadraticForm.equivalent_signType_weighted_sum_squared` in `Mathlib/LinearAlgebra/QuadraticForm/Real.lean`). A search of the Mathlib sources for `Courant`, `min_max` and `inertia` found no min-max theorem for matrices and no statement that the number of negative squares does not depend on the diagonalization. Comparing characteristic polynomials (Mathlib's `Matrix.IsHermitian.roots_charpoly_eq_eigenvalues`) would have covered exact similarity, but not the inequalities between counts that Lemma 4 and Weyl's inequality need. The library therefore proves the subspace fact once, in `QuadForm.lean` and `Inertia.lean`, and derives the rest from it. A min-max theorem in Mathlib would have made this part shorter; nothing else in the plan depended on it.

The argument in `QuadForm.lean` uses no eigenvalues. If `M = L diag(d) Lᴴ` with `Lᴴ` invertible, the map `x ↦ Lᴴ x` carries the form of `M` to the form of `diag(d)`; on the coordinate subspace where `d < 0` that form is negative, on the one where `d ≥ 0` it is non-negative, and two subspaces that meet only in `0` have dimensions adding to at most `n` (`finrank_add_finrank_le`). `Inertia.lean` applies this to the spectral decomposition `A - t I = U diag(λ - t) Uᴴ`.

## Conventions

Counting form and sorted form. Mathlib lists the eigenvalues of `A` as a function `hA.eigenvalues : n → ℝ` with no order on `n` (its sorted list `eigenvalues₀` is decreasing). The core theorems are stated through the counts `cntLt hA t = #{i | λ i < t}` and `cntLe hA t = #{i | λ i ≤ t}`. `Sorted.lean` defines the eigenvalues in increasing order, `eigAsc hA j = eigenvalues₀ (Fin.rev j)` for `j : Fin n` (so `eigAsc hA j` is the `(j+1)`-th smallest eigenvalue, counted with multiplicity), and proves the translation: `eigAsc hA j ≤ x` exactly when `j + 1 ≤ cntLe hA x` (`eigAsc_le_iff`), and `y ≤ eigAsc hA j` exactly when `cntLt hA y ≤ j` (`le_eigAsc_iff`). `eigAsc_mem_of_counts` turns the two halves of a pairing in counting form into an interval for `eigAsc`. The statements of the documents about "the j-th eigenvalue" (Weyl's inequality, Corollary 5 and its model transfer, Theorem 8, E-04 and Proposition 2 of E-07) are stated in both forms.

Norm hypotheses. The core theorems take a perturbation `E` through the bound `|xᴴ E x| ≤ β ‖x‖²` for all `x`. The documents state `‖E‖₂ ≤ β`. The first follows from the second by the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality; this step is kernel-checked in `Norms.lean` (`abs_qf_le_of_toEuclideanLin`), for the operator form `‖E z‖ ≤ β ‖z‖` on `EuclideanSpace 𝕜 n` that Mathlib and `ENCLOSURE.md` use, and Lemma 4, Weyl's inequality and Theorem 8 are restated there with the norm hypotheses of the documents. The spectral norm of a matrix `M` is written `‖M‖` below and is, in Lean, the operator norm of `Matrix.toEuclideanLin M`, used through its defining bound. For a Hermitian `E` the quadratic-form bound also follows from the largest absolute row sum (`abs_qf_le_rowSum`), which is the route of D-01.

Floating-point model. The floating-point statements (parts (a) to (c) of D-01, the corollary and Lemmas A and B of Proposition 15) are stated for a rounding `R : Rounding ℝ` of `CertifyMathlib.Enclosure.FloatModel` with `R.eta = 0`: every operation returns the exact result times `1 + δ` with `|δ| ≤ u`, with no underflow, which is the standard model of the documents (Higham 2002, eq. 2.4). The unit roundoff is a hypothesis `u ≤ 2⁻⁵³` (D-01) or `u ≤ 2⁻¹⁰` (Proposition 15). Complex operations are the componentwise roundings `crnd` and the products `cmul` (four real products and two real sums) of `FloatAccounting`. Every sum the code forms is evaluated along an arbitrary summation tree (`STree`), so the statements hold for any order and association of the additions. A constant such as `1 + 4u` or `1 + 8u`, which is a binary64 number, enters exactly; every product by it is rounded; `gamma(k)`, the floating-point value of `γ_k = k u / (1 - k u)` that the code computes, is `gamHat R k` with `γ_k (1 - u)² ≤ gamHat R k` (`gamHat_bounds`, from `FloatAccounting`); `numpy.nextafter(x, +∞)`, the step of `fp.add_up`, is any function `succ` with `z ≤ succ (fl z)`.

## Correspondence, statement by statement

### Sylvester's law of inertia

Document: if `A = L D Lᴴ` with `L` invertible and `D` real diagonal, the number of negative eigenvalues of `A` is the number of negative entries of `D` (`theory.md` §3; used in Step 5 of `code/certify-ed/docs/proofs/D-01_sparse-residual.md`). Lean: `sylvester_inertia` gives `cntLt hA 0 = #{i | d i < 0}` and `cntLe hA 0 = #{i | d i ≤ 0}`, so the numbers of zero and of positive eigenvalues agree as well. `cntLt_eq_of_factor` is the same at a shift `t` with a rectangular `L` that has a two-sided inverse, and `sylvester_card` compares two such factorizations of one matrix without any eigenvalue. The statement is the document's.

### Min-max count and Weyl's inequality

Document: the counts of `theory.md` §3 rest on the min-max characterization, and Step 4 of `cor5_pairing.md` and Steps 3 and 4 of `thm8_rho.md` use Weyl's inequality `|λ_j(A) - λ_j(B)| ≤ ρ` for Hermitian `A`, `B` with `‖A - B‖ ≤ ρ`. Lean: `finrank_le_cntLt` and `exists_negOn_cntLt` say that `cntLt hA t` is the largest dimension of a subspace on which `xᴴ (A - t I) x < 0` (and `finrank_le_cntLe`, `exists_nonposOn_cntLe` the same for `≤ 0` and `cntLe`). `weyl_cntLt` and `weyl_cntLe` give `cntLt hA t ≤ cntLt hB (t + ρ)` and the same for `cntLe` under the quadratic-form bound on `A - B`, `weyl_opNorm` under `‖A - B‖ ≤ ρ`; `weyl_sorted` and `weyl_sorted_opNorm` give the sorted form `|eigAsc hA j - eigAsc hB j| ≤ ρ`. The statements are the document's.

### Lemma 4, inertia with backward error

Document: if `‖L D Lᴴ - P(A - σ I)Pᵀ‖ ≤ β` for unit lower-triangular `L` and block-diagonal `D` with `ν` negative eigenvalues, then `#{λ < σ - β} ≤ ν ≤ #{λ ≤ σ + β}` (`theory.md` §3). The manuscript states the strict upper count `ν ≤ #{λ < σ + β}` (`lem:inertia` in `manuscript_v4/sections/s3_math.tex`). Lean: `lemma4` proves the strict form for any `L` whose conjugate transpose has a two-sided inverse and diagonal `D`, with `E` given by `L D Lᴴ = A - σ I + E` and the quadratic-form bound on `E`; `lemma4_doc` gives the `theory.md` form for square invertible `L`; `lemma4_opNorm` takes `‖E‖₂ ≤ β`; `lemma4_hermitian` allows any Hermitian `D` (so a block-diagonal `D` with 1 × 1 and 2 × 2 blocks) with `ν` the number of its negative eigenvalues; `lemma4_perm` allows the permutation, as any unitary `P`. Unit triangularity is not needed, only invertibility. `cntLt_fin_two` is the rule by which the code counts a 2 × 2 block: negative determinant, one negative eigenvalue; positive determinant, two or none according to the sign of the trace. Together with `cnt_blockDiagonal'` it gives `ν` of a block-diagonal `D` as the sum over its blocks.

### Corollary 5, completeness and sorted pairing

Document: `code/certify-ed/docs/proofs/cor5_pairing.md`, section 1 (hypotheses H1 to H3, conclusions for `A_u` and, with the model shift `Δ`, for the declared model `A_m`). Lean: the claimed values `μ` and radii `r` are indexed by a finite type `ι` with `k` elements. (H1) is formalized as a group map `grp : ι → G`, radii constant on each group, group hulls `[lo g, hi g]` containing each member's interval and pairwise disjoint, and an injection `φ` of the claims into the eigen-indices with `|λ (φ j) - μ j| ≤ r j`; `injective_of_groups` builds this injection from injections on the groups (Step 1). (H2) is `cntLt hA c ≤ k` at the cut `c = σ - β`, the lower half of Lemma 4 with `ν = k`. (H3) is `μ j + r j < c`. `image_eq_of_match` gives Step 2 (exactly `k` eigenvalues below `c`, and they are the matched ones, so `λ_{k+1} ≥ c`); `cor5_le` and `cor5_lt` give the pairing in counting form; `cor5_sorted` restates it for sorted claimed values `μ : Fin k → ℝ` as `j + 1 ≤ cntLe hA (μ j + r j)` and `cntLt hA (μ j - r j) ≤ j`, which is `λ_{j+1} ∈ [μ_j - r_j, μ_j + r_j]`; `cor5_model` is Step 4 with the document's (H3) `μ_j + r_j + Δ < σ - β - Δ`, and gives exactly `k` eigenvalues of `A_m` below `σ - β - Δ` and the pairing for `A_m` with radii `r_j + Δ`. The manuscript's two sufficient conditions (enclosures pairwise disjoint, or a common radius) are the cases "every claim its own group" and "one group". The gap bound of the document is the difference of the two bounds and is not stated separately. In `Sorted.lean`, `cor5_eigAsc` gives the sorted form `|eigAsc hA j - μ_j| ≤ r_j` for `j < k` and `eigAsc hA k ≥ c`, and `cor5_model_eigAsc` gives `|eigAsc hAm j - μ_j| ≤ r_j + Δ` and `eigAsc hAm k ≥ σ - β - Δ` for the declared model.

### D-01, a-posteriori backward error

Document: `code/certify-ed/docs/proofs/D-01_sparse-residual.md`, with the code `sparse_ldl_residual_bound` in `code/certify-ed/certify_ed/ldl_residual.py` (lines 86 to 163). Parts (a) to (c) bound the exact residual `E = L D Lᴴ - S_P` of the stored factors through the floating-point quantities the code forms (Steps 1 to 4); Lemma R1 bounds `‖E‖₂` by `‖E‖_∞` for Hermitian `E`; Step 5 adds the rounding of the shifted diagonal and applies Lemma 4.

Exact part (`Lemma4.lean`). `abs_qf_le_rowSum` is Lemma R1 in quadratic-form form, and `lemma4_rowSum` is Step 5: with a diagonal rounding `|Δ_i| ≤ s` and a Hermitian residual of absolute row sums at most `R`, Lemma 4 holds with `β = R + s`. `lemma4_perm_rowSum` (in `D01Float.lean`) is the same with the permutation, as any unitary `P`, for `L D Lᴴ = P (A - σ I + diag Δ) Pᴴ + E`.

Parts (a) and (b) (`D01Float.lean`). The model is the one of the conventions above. The products of entry `(i, j)` are summed along a tree `T i j` whose leaves are distinct indices `k`, every omitted `k` having `L_ik = 0` or `L_jk = 0` (`PatternTrees`); `t_ij` is the number of its leaves. For real data `prodFl` is `F_c,ij`, the sum along `T i j` of `fl(L_ik fl(D_k L_jk))`, and `residFl` is `E_c,ij = fl(F_c,ij - S_P,ij)`. `d01_a` is (a): `|E_ij| ≤ |E_c,ij| / (1 - u) + γ_{w+1} G_ij` for every `w ≥ t_ij` with `(w + 1) u < 1`, `G = |L| |D| |L|ᵀ` (`Gabs`); with `w = t_ij` this is the document's bound with `g_{t_ij+1} = γ_{t_ij+1}`. `d01_b` is (b) as absolute row sums, `Σ_j |E_ij| ≤ Σ_j (|E_c,ij| / (1 - u) + γ_{c_i+1} G_ij)` when `t_ij ≤ c_i`; with Lemma R1 it bounds `‖E‖₂`. Part (c), real data (`D01Float.lean`, section `PartC`). ### Spectrum of a block-diagonal matrix and Theorem 8

The code uses the inequality only with `J ⊂ I_in` (line 367), which implies the hypothesis, so no certified statement is affected; the display on line 365 needs the condition "if `σ_lo + β_lo < σ_hi − β_hi`".

The upper count of Lemma 4. The Lean proof gives the strict count `ν ≤ #{λ < σ + β}` without extra hypotheses, as the manuscript states it (`manuscript_v4/sections/s3_math.tex`, `lem:inertia`). The `theory.md` form with `≤` follows from it. Nothing in the code depends on the difference.

The separation in Theorem 6. `thm:dk` measures the separation from the Ritz values; the sin Θ theorem in its usual form (and `sinTheta`, `thm6_proj`) measures it from an interval that contains the values with respect to which the residual is formed. `thm6_ritz` states the theorem with an interval containing the Ritz values, which under the hypotheses of `thm:dk` is the manuscript's statement: the remaining eigenvalues lie outside `I` and the Ritz values inside, so the distance from the Ritz values equals the distance from their hull. The two-line argument of the manuscript that the Ritz values lie inside `I` is not formalized; `thm6_ritz` takes the location of the Ritz values as its hypothesis.

The constants of Theorem 8. The manuscript's `thm:compose` has no model term; `thm8_rho.md` adds the model bound `m_b`. `thm8_polar` has both: with `ε = ρ + m_b` it gives `r + ε` and `E_cut - max β - ε`, and `m_b = 0` gives the manuscript's statement with `ρ` of `lem:polar`.

Traces without the trace norm. The proofs of C-04, C-07 and C-13 in the documents bound `|tr(E O)|` by `‖E‖₁ ‖O‖₂` (Hölder's inequality for the trace) and `‖E‖₁` by the rank of `E` times `‖E‖₂`. Mathlib `v4.35.0` has neither the trace norm nor this inequality, and the Lean proofs of these statements and of the trace bound of Theorem 6 use the rank bound directly (`norm_trace_mul_le`, `norm_trace_herm_le`). The constants are the documents': for `E = X Xᴴ - P` the rank is at most `g + #T`, which is `2g` when `#T = g`. C-07 Lemma 1 holds for any matrix `M`, as the document uses it.

The complex constant of D-01 (a). For complex data the Lean model gives `γ_{t_ij+3}` in (a), below the document's `g_{t_ij+1} = 2 γ_{t_ij+5}` (the code's `gamma_dot`); the document's constant is the more conservative of the two, and the code is unaffected.

The case `s_DK ≥ 1` of E-07 Proposition 1. `e07_prop1_proj` assumes `s_DK < 1`. For `s_DK ≥ 1` the bound `‖Q̂ Q̂ᴴ - P‖ ≤ s_DK` holds because two orthogonal projectors differ by at most 1 in norm; that case is not stated in Lean.

The sorted pairing without the exchange argument. In counting form, Corollary 5 needs no exchange of an inversion: each of `cor5_le` and `cor5_lt` compares two counts at one threshold and uses the equality of radii within a group exactly once. This is where the remark of `cor5_pairing.md` (the pairing fails for non-uniform radii) enters the proof. The second derivation recorded in `VERDICTS.md` also used a counting argument.

The sampling criterion of E-04. `sampling_criterion` needs the margin to be `2B`-Lipschitz on the whole sample interval. At an interior grid point the tubes can jump by the affinity defects, and there the hypothesis fails; this is the case in which the second derivation found the error recorded in `VERDICTS.md` for `E-04_parameter_intervals.md` (fixed in the code by adding the defects to the slack). The Lean statement makes the hypothesis explicit.

## How the files were checked

Each file was elaborated with `lake env lean` until it gave no error, then built as a module. The last build of the root module, `lake build CertifyMathlib.Counting`, completed with exit code 0 ("Build completed successfully (2763 jobs)"); the only warnings from the counting files are the notices that `QuadForm.lean` and `Theorem6.lean` import Mathlib modules designed for the module system. The 22 files (the root module and 21 modules) contain no `sorry`, no `admit`, no `native_decide`, no `axiom` declaration and no `set_option`; this was checked by searching the files. `#print axioms` was run for every theorem by `lean/Drafts/Counting/PrintAxioms.lean`; every one of the 291 theorems depends exactly on `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound` (the output is kept in `lean/Drafts/Counting/print_axioms_output.txt`).

The second checker of the toolchain, `leanchecker`, replayed every module in one of four runs and accepted it (exit code 0, no message). Every theorem of the 22 files is therefore at least at level K+. The runs:

| run | modules replayed | exit code | seconds |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `QuadForm`, `Inertia`, `Lemma4`, `Completeness`, `Assembly`, `Thermo`, `Eigenspace`, `Parameter`, `Norms` and the root module before `Variance` was added | 0 | 2091.1 |
| 2 | `Variance` and the root module with its import | 0 | 875.6 |
| 3 | `Compose`, `DavisKahan`, `Theorem6`, `E07`, `Ostrowski`, `Truncated`, `Observables`, `Sorted`, `Labels` and the root module before the floating-point modules were added | 0 | 2101.7 |
| 4 | `D01Float`, `P15Float` and the root module in its final form | 0 | 1316.6 |

The independent kernel `nanoda` (the `nanoda_bin` of the toolchain, reading the export of `leanexport`) was run on the 71 main theorems, one theorem per run, by `lean/Drafts/Counting/nanoda_counting.py`: `leanexport <module> -- <theorem>` writes the theorem with every declaration it depends on (the Lean core and Mathlib included), and `nanoda_bin` checks the export with the permitted axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`. A run was to be stopped above 4 GiB of private memory or 30 minutes; none was. All 71 were accepted, none rejected, none left unrun, so each of the 25 statements that are proved or partial is at level K2; for a partial statement the level concerns its formalized part. Every run printed "Checked N declarations with no typechecker errors, 1 pretty printer errors" with the pretty printer error "Unable to print axioms", which concerns the printing of the axiom list, not type checking. The exports had 6955 to 36149 declarations and 25.9 to 211.2 MB; `leanexport` needed 3877 to 4023 MiB of private memory and `nanoda_bin` at most 384 MiB; the runs took 10318.8 seconds in total. The result of every run is in the results file.
